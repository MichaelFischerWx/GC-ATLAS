Lorenz energy cycle · stationary eddies

Lorenz energy cycle (stationary-eddy form). Four reservoirs, four conversions. Reservoirs in MJ m⁻², conversions in W m⁻², all mass-weighted vertically over the 12 pressure levels and area-weighted globally (cosφ).

Reservoirs — available potential + kinetic energy, mean + eddy:

P_M = (R/2g) ∫ T̂² / (γ p) dp        (mean APE)
P_E = (R/2g) ∫ ⟨T*²⟩ / (γ p) dp     (eddy APE)
K_M = (1/2g) ∫ ([u]² + [v]²) dp     (mean KE)
K_E = (1/2g) ∫ ⟨u*² + v*²⟩ dp       (eddy KE)

where T̂ = [T] − T_ref (zonal-mean departure from reference), T* = T − [T] (stationary-eddy departure from zonal mean), γ(p) = κT_ref/p − ∂T_ref/∂p (static stability), [·] is zonal mean, ⟨·⟩ is global area-mean and ( )″ the departure from it. Leading factor R/(2g) is the Holton–Hakim / Vallis form (using c_p/2g instead overshoots by ~3.5×).

Conversions — each has a clean physical reading:

C(P_M→P_E) = -(R/g) ∫ ⟨v*T*⟩/(γp) · ∂[T]/∂y dp
              -(R/g) ∫ ⟨ω*T*⟩ p^-κ ·
                 ∂(p^κ T̂/(γp))/∂p dp
   ► baroclinic release — eddy heat flux
     down the mean T gradient

C(P_E→K_E) = -(R/g) ∫ ⟨ω*T*⟩ / p dp
   ► eddy buoyancy flux — warm air rising,
     cold air sinking in the eddies

C(K_E→K_M) =  (1/g) ∫ ⟨u*v*⟩ cosφ · ∂([u]/cosφ)/∂y dp
              +(1/g) ∫ ⟨u*ω*⟩ · ∂[u]/∂p dp
   ► eddy momentum flux spinning up the
     zonal-mean jets (barotropic)

C(P_M→K_M) = -(R/g) ∫ ⟨[ω]″[T]″⟩ / p dp
   ► zonal-mean meridional circulation:
     direct Hadley cells (+) against
     indirect Ferrel cells (−); they
     nearly cancel globally

Implementation notes:

  • Inputs are the monthly-mean u, v, ω, T fields on 12 pressure levels (10 → 1000 hPa) from ERA5.
  • Vertical integration uses each level's half-distance layer (dp = (p_{k-1} + p_{k+1})/2 − (p_{k-1} + p_k)/2), one-sided at top and bottom.
  • Meridional derivatives ∂/∂y use centered differences on a = 6.371·10⁶ m; vertical ∂/∂p likewise on the 12-level pressure grid.
  • Global ⟨·⟩ uses a cosφ area weight over the full (181 × 360) grid. Pressure levels below the surface (p > p_s, the monthly-mean surface pressure) are left out, following Boer (1982): [·] averages the above-ground part of each latitude circle, and ⟨·⟩ weights each latitude by that part's share but divides by the whole sphere, so ∫dp/g is a mass integral over the air that's actually there.

Reference-state toggle (controls T_ref, which sets γ — the 1/(γp) weight in P_M, P_E and C(P_M, P_E) — and the T̂ in P_M and the vertical part of C(P_M, P_E)):

  • Lorenz (sorted) — the true minimum-PE reference from Lorenz (1955). Every above-ground parcel (~730k on the 12-level globe, each carrying its layer's mass, the lowest one's layer reaching down to the surface) is adiabatically re-stratified by θ, highest θ at the top, into one column from 10 hPa down to the global-mean surface pressure (~986 hPa). T_ref at a level is the mass-weighted mean over the slab of that column the level stands for. This is the canonical "available" formulation. The coldest ~2 % of the mass (winter-continent and polar boundary-layer air) pools at the bottom, so this reference is very stable below ~900 hPa: γp/T ≈ 0.5–0.6 at 925 hPa against ~0.1 in the free troposphere. That is the minimum-PE state itself (the sorted column sampled finely gives the same), and it is why the lowest layers hold less P_M here than under the area mean.
  • Area-mean — T_ref(p) = global area-weighted mean of T at each pressure level. The textbook diagnostic approximation. Faster, but it overstates how much PE is "available" — a lot of the meridional T gradient it sees is actually unavailable (non-adiabatic to release). It averages the whole isobaric surface, ERA5's below-ground values included: over the air alone, the 1000 hPa mean covers only the warm low-lying two-thirds of the globe and comes out superadiabatic against 925 hPa.

Why sorted ≠ area-mean: both formulations share u, v, ω, T — only T_ref (and hence T̂, γ) differ. The sorted reference is colder and far more stable in the lower troposphere, so the same temperature departure there is worth less APE: P_M comes out ~13 % smaller (12-month mean 3.5 vs 4.0 MJ m⁻²) and P_E ~25 % smaller, and C(P_M, P_E) shifts from its meridional to its vertical term (annual 0.21 + 0.11 sorted vs 0.28 + 0.04 W m⁻² area-mean) at about the same total. That is Lorenz's (1955) point in visible form: part of the pole-to-equator contrast the area mean counts can't be released adiabatically. C(P_M, K_M) is identical in both panels: in Peixoto & Oort's form ⟨[ω]″[T]″⟩ the reference drops out.

Left column: Peixoto & Oort (1992) Table 13.1 for the full circulation. Right column: the lowest to highest monthly value of the 1991-2020 climatology here, sorted reference (area-mean where it differs). The stationary eddies peak in Dec-Jan and are weakest in Mar-May. The mean-state terms compare directly: P_M with P&O's 4.0 and Li et al.'s (2007) 4.3 MJ/m², whose formulas take the isobaric mean as reference (12-month mean here: 4.0 area-mean, 3.5 sorted), and K_M with 0.8. The eddy terms here are stationary only; Boer (1989) puts that share at ~15-30% of the total. For C(P_M, P_E), Li et al. (2007) find 1.79 W/m² (ERA-40; NCEP2 1.69) for the global annual mean with this same split — stationary = monthly-mean departures from the zonal mean, plus sub-monthly transients. 15-30% of that is 0.27-0.54; the 12-month mean here is 0.33 (area-mean 0.32). C(P_M, K_M) is a near-cancellation: 30°S-30°N (the direct Hadley cells) gives +0.5 to +0.7 W/m² and poleward of 30° (mostly the indirect Ferrel cells) −0.5 to −0.7, leaving −0.07 for the 12-month mean, so its sign is fragile. Reanalysis-to-reanalysis variance alone is ±15-20%, so matches within that range count as good.

Caveats:

  • Stationary eddies only. * here is the departure of the monthly-mean field from its zonal mean — it captures planetary waves locked to topography / land-sea contrast. High-frequency (sub-monthly) transient eddies, which dominate baroclinic conversion in published totals, need daily data (currently out of scope).
  • C(P_M, P_E) includes the vertical eddy heat flux in Peixoto & Oort's (1974) form: ⟨ω*T*⟩ acts on the vertical structure of T̂, the departure from the reference state, not on ∂[T]/∂p. The eddy heat flux against the reference lapse rate itself is the eddy buoyancy flux, already counted in C(P_E, K_E). By month the vertical term is 0.07-0.16 W/m² here under the sorted reference (0.01-0.09 area-mean), against 0.08-0.43 (0.10-0.58) for the meridional one; with transients included, Li et al. (2007) find the ω terms ~10× smaller than the horizontal ones.
  • Below ground. ERA5 fills pressure levels under the surface with extrapolated values (a third of the 1000 hPa surface, a tenth of 925 hPa). Their ω is no circulation (+0.02 to +0.10 Pa/s on average at 700-1000 hPa, against < 0.001 over the air there); with the extrapolated T it would add +0.9 (Jan) to +1.3 W/m² (Jul) of spurious C(P_M, K_M), in July nearly all of it south of 60°S, and a third of July's C(P_M, P_E). Those cells are left out everywhere, the sort included.
  • Expect ~3-6× smaller conversions than Peixoto-Oort "total" numbers because transients are excluded — that gap is the transient-eddy contribution, not a bug.

Use the cycle to illustrate structure — which arrows are large, which way energy flows, how seasonal cycles shift it (swipe through months). Toggle the reference state to see how much of the area-mean APE is unavailable (P_M −13 %, P_E −25 % under the sorted reference) — that's the pedagogy in its own right.

Zonal-mean cross-section

Stationary-eddy angular momentum budget. Per unit mass:

∂[M]/∂t = -(1/(a cosφ))·∂([v][M] cosφ)/∂y - ∂([ω][M])/∂p   mean transport
          -(1/(a cosφ))·∂([v*M*] cosφ)/∂y - ∂([ω*M*])/∂p   stationary eddies
          + F_λ · a cosφ                                    friction + torque

where M = (Ω a cosφ + [u])·a cosφ. [·] = zonal mean, * = stationary eddy departure (M* = a cosφ · u*). In monthly steady-state ∂[M]/∂t ≈ 0, so the implied surface torque (residual) is shown as the negative of the sum.

Display variable: ∂[u]/∂t (m s⁻¹ day⁻¹) = ∂[M]/∂t / (a cosφ) — the implied zonal-wind tendency. Or ∂[M]/∂t scaled to 10⁶ m² s⁻¹ day⁻¹.

Aggregation modes:

  • (lat, p) — full cross-section heatmap (Holton Fig 10.4 style).
  • col-mean — mass-weighted vertical mean (1/p_s)·∫T·dp. Same units as 2D, "what the column-average tendency looks like."
  • ∫dp/g — vertical integral ∫T·(1/g)·dp in N/m² for ∂[u]/∂t form. Directly comparable to surface friction stress (~0.1 N/m²). This is the canonical Peixoto–Oort Fig 11.6 form.

Stationary eddies only. The transient-eddy contribution (sub-monthly variability) is excluded. Per Peixoto–Oort 1992 Table 11.1, stationary [u*v*] is roughly 40% of total in NH winter at 200 hPa (12 vs 30 m²/s²). For SH or annual mean the stationary fraction is smaller. Implied torque magnitudes here will be off vs. published totals by the missing transient amount.

Below ground. ERA5 fills pressure levels under the surface with extrapolated values (a third of the 1000 hPa surface). They are left out, following Boer (1982): [·] and the eddy covariances average the above-ground part of each latitude circle, and a level with less than a quarter of its circle above ground is hatched. The column views weight each level by the air it holds, down to the surface, so ∫dp/g is the zonal mean of the column integrals (comparable with the friction and mountain lines) and the column mean divides by [p_s] rather than 1000 hPa. With the below-ground cells, the January ∫dp/g total was −0.9 to −1.4 N/m² at 30–40°N and −2.0 (Jan) to −3.5 N/m² (Jul) near 75°S; without them it is within ±0.2 N/m² there.

Friction + mountain overlays (all-terms 1D view, when the ews, sp and oro tiles are built) — zonal-mean forces per unit area on the air, positive when they add westerly momentum (Peixoto & Oort 1992, ch. 11; Newton 1971):

friction  F_f = −[τ_E]       τ_E = ERA5 ews: stress of the air on the surface
mountain  F_m = −[p_s ∂h/∂x] = −[p_s ∂h/∂λ] / (a cosφ)

Higher surface pressure on a mountain's west slope than on its east slope pushes the air westward (F_m < 0, like friction under the westerlies). The ∂[u]/∂t form plots F; the ∂[M]/∂t form plots the torque per unit area T = F · a cosφ (so T_m = −[p_s ∂h/∂λ]); col-mean spreads them over the column mass p_s/g, ∫dp/g gives F in N/m² or T in 10⁶ kg s⁻². Globally they nearly cancel in the annual mean (friction −0.95, mountain +0.84 × 10¹⁹ N m for 1991–2020), as a steady AAM requires. In steady state implied torque − (friction + mountain) ≈ transient-eddy convergence (+ parametrized gravity-wave drag, which ews leaves out) — a direct measure of what monthly means approximate away.

References: Newton (1971); Newell et al. (1972, 1974); Oort & Peixoto (1983 atlas); Peixoto & Oort (1992) ch. 11; Holton (textbook) ch. 10; Egger, Weickmann & Hoinka (2007, Rev. Geophys.) for the modern AAM review.

Mean meridional mass streamfunction, positive in the sense of the NH Hadley cell — rising near the equator, poleward aloft, sinking in the subtropics (clockwise as drawn, pressure increasing downward):

ψ(φ, p) = (2π a cosφ / g) ∫₀ᵖ [βv] dp′

Below ground. ERA5 fills pressure levels under the surface with extrapolated values. [βv] sums v over the cells above the surface and divides by the whole circle (Boer 1982), so ψ is the mass flux through the part of the latitude circle that is open. The extrapolated winds had drawn a cell of their own around Antarctica: ψ = +45 (Jan) to +73 × 10⁹ kg s⁻¹ (Jul) at 72°S, 1000 hPa, as strong as the Ferrel cells. A level with less than a quarter of its circle above ground is hatched: 1000 hPa poleward of ~55°S (the circumpolar trough's surface pressure is below 1000 hPa all year) and the levels under the Antarctic plateau. The integral still counts their sliver of air.

Stationary-eddy Eliassen–Palm flux, quasi-geostrophic form in pressure coordinates (Edmon, Hoskins & McIntyre 1980):

F_φ = −a cosφ [u*v*]
F_p =  a cosφ f [v*θ*] / (∂θ/∂p)
∇·F = (1/(a cosφ)) ∂(F_φ cosφ)/∂φ + ∂F_p/∂p

Shading is ∇·F / (a cosφ), the eddy forcing of [u] in m s⁻¹ day⁻¹: blue (convergence) decelerates the westerlies. Its meridional part is the eddy momentum-flux convergence of the M budget. Arrows are scaled for the (φ, log p) aspect of the panel; only their directions and relative lengths carry meaning. * = departure from the zonal mean of the monthly-mean field, so these are stationary eddies only: the transient storm-track fluxes are missing.

Below ground. ERA5 fills pressure levels under the surface with extrapolated values (a third of the 1000 hPa surface). They are left out, following Boer (1982): [·], the eddy covariances and the stratification average the above-ground part of each latitude circle, and a level with less than a quarter of its circle above ground is hatched — 1000 hPa poleward of ~55°S, where the circumpolar trough's surface pressure is below 1000 hPa all year, and the levels under the Antarctic plateau. Because a partly blocked circle has its air at different longitudes on different levels, ∂θ/∂p is the average of each column's own stratification, and ∂F_p/∂p compares the two levels over the same columns. The extrapolated cells had made −3 (Jan) to −8 m s⁻¹ day⁻¹ (Jul) of spurious convergence at 700 hPa, 70–75°S, and set the July colour scale (−21 at 1000 hPa, 82°S).

Lowest levels: where the air is near-neutral (the wintertime marine surface layer), ∂θ/∂p is small and F_p large, as in any QG EP flux. Near 40°N in January the convergence reaches −13 m s⁻¹ day⁻¹ at 925 hPa (−5 before the below-ground cells' stable extrapolated profiles were dropped); read the bottom two rows qualitatively. Next to the Antarctic plateau, where 700 hPa is the lowest level with air, the same goes for 500 hPa: −5 to −8 at 70–80°S in July, against −0.6 to +0.5 computed on all 37 ERA5 levels.

Top levels: there is no level above 10 hPa and none between 10 and 50 hPa, so ∂θ/∂p and ∂F_p/∂p at 10 hPa are one-sided across that gap. The secant makes ∂θ/∂p 3½× too small and F_p 3½× too large, and the row shows divergence where there is convergence: in January +6.6 m s⁻¹ day⁻¹ at 60°N and +14.8 at 72°N, against −6.0 and −2.3 on all 37 levels. The 50 hPa row differences across 10–100 hPa and carries part of the error (+1 to +3 at 60–80°N; −2 to +1 on 37 levels). Read the top two rows qualitatively.

Stationary-eddy moisture (Q) and moist-static-energy (H) budgets. Per unit mass, for X = q or h = c_pT + gz + L_v q:

∂[X]/∂t = −(1/(a cosφ)) ∂([v][X] cosφ)/∂φ − ∂([ω][X])/∂p   mean
          −(1/(a cosφ)) ∂([v*X*] cosφ)/∂φ − ∂([ω*X*])/∂p   stationary eddies
          + source

In a steady month the implied source (−total) is E − P for q (the E and P lines) and the column's heating for h (LH + SH, plus net radiation where the tree has tsr). The column views compare ∫dp/g with those surface lines. Stationary eddies only: the transient fluxes are missing, most of the midlatitude eddy transport.

Mass correction. Monthly-mean v and ω on pressure levels don't conserve mass (ψ doesn't close at the ground: 35 × 10⁹ kg/s at 1000 hPa on the equator in January), and in flux form each mean term carries [X] times the divergence of the mean mass flux. With h ≈ 3 × 10⁵ J/kg that was hundreds of W/m² in the column: poleward of 80°S the July ∫dp/g total ran from −640 to +1340 W/m², single terms to ±2900. The mean circulation is now made to conserve mass, as Trenberth (1991, 1997) does for column transports: [v] plus the barotropic wind that takes the net mass flux across the latitude circle to zero, and [ω] from continuity, as for ψ. Away from the ground this is ERA5's circulation (the two [ω] differ by the imbalance only), and the two mean terms add up to the advective form, so their sum is meaningful level by level. The H total is now ±1–2 K/day, where it was ±8–10.

Column views. The terms are fluxes between air layers (each level stands for the layer between the midpoints to its neighbours, down to the surface in each column; [·] is the mass-weighted mean over the layer's air), so ∫dp/g of the meridional terms is exactly the convergence of the mean-circulation and stationary-eddy transports of the Meridional transport panel, and the vertical terms integrate to zero (the all-terms column plots leave them out). One difference: that panel lets its MMC carry the net vapour flux (Trenberth 1997); here the net mass flux is zero. The energy a net mass flux carries depends on the zero of the energy scale and is balanced only by the enthalpy of the water crossing the surface, which LH + SH + radiation leave out, so with the zero target the totals compare with those lines directly. The two MMC convergences differ by ≈ Ē (E − P): up to ~15 W/m² in the deep tropics, ≤ 6 W/m² poleward of 30°.

Check. With the transient fluxes of 6-hourly ERA5 for 2000 added (WeatherBench2, not yet in the atlas), mean + stationary + transient convergence balances −(LH + SH + radiation) and the storage over the polar night to 16 W/m² rms in July (Antarctica; 512 before the correction) and 19 W/m² in January (Arctic; 113 before). The stationary terms supply 41 % and 7 % of it, the transients most of the rest.

Below ground. ERA5 fills pressure levels under the surface with extrapolated values. They are left out, following Boer (1982), and a level with less than a quarter of its latitude circle above ground is hatched. With them the January H-budget ∫dp/g total was −700 to −1170 W/m² at 30–40°N and −1700 to −4000 W/m² at 70–75°S (−6500 at 75°S in July); it is now within ±65 W/m² there.

Read the mean terms together. Each alone still holds [X] times its own half of the (now balanced) mass divergence: in the boundary layer under the ITCZ and against the Antarctic slopes they reach ±140 K/day, of opposite sign. Near the poles the latitude bands are so small that the column numbers are noisy: the cap poleward of 88° is 0.03 % of the globe, so a 0.01 PW error in the transport across 88° is 65 W/m² there. The 1-D y-range comes from within 85° of the equator; a curve that leaves it stops at the frame, and hovering reads it.

Absolute angular momentum per unit mass of the zonal-mean flow:

M = (Ω a cosφ + [u]) a cosφ

A ring of air that left the equator at rest and kept its M would have u = Ω a sin²φ / cosφ, 134 m s⁻¹ at 30°: where the contours of M tilt away from the vertical, M is not conserved.

Below ground. [u] averages the above-ground part of each latitude circle (Boer 1982); a level with less than a quarter of its circle above ground is hatched. Near the ground [u] moves by up to 2 m s⁻¹ equatorward of 60° (January, 40°N, 1000 hPa: 2.6 → 3.9), which changes M by at most 0.012 × 10⁹ m² s⁻¹, 0.5 %: Ω a cosφ is hundreds of m s⁻¹.

Zonal-mean geostrophic wind, blank within 5° of the equator (f → 0):

[u_g] = −(g/f) [∂z/∂y]

Compare with the field section of u: they agree in the extratropical free troposphere; near the ground friction slows [u] below [u_g].

Below ground. Each column with air at the level takes its own ∂z/∂y (one-sided where the point north or south of it is underground), and [u_g] is their mean over the air, the columns the field section's [u] averages. A level with less than a quarter of its circle above ground is hatched. Differencing the air-part [z] between latitudes would compare different longitudes; at 500–850 hPa, 25–45°N in July it departs from the air-part [u] by 1.1 m s⁻¹ rms, the column mean by 0.26 (the old all-circle panel: 0.55). At 1000 hPa, 35–40°N in January the extrapolated heights had made [u_g] 2.1–2.2 m s⁻¹, slower than [u] (2.6–2.7); over the air it is 4.7–4.8 against [u] 3.8–3.9. At 925 hPa, 70°S in January: −4.4 → −1.8 m s⁻¹ ([u] −1.7).

Static stability of the zonal-mean state, in 10⁻⁴ s⁻² (troposphere ≈ 1, stratosphere ≈ 4):

N² = (g/θ) ∂θ/∂z = −(g² p / (R T θ)) ∂θ/∂p

Centred differences in p, one-sided at 10 and 1000 hPa.

Below ground. ∂θ/∂p is the mean over the air of each column's own ∂θ/∂p (one-sided where the level below is underground in that column); T and θ are air-part zonal means; a level with less than a quarter of its circle above ground is hatched. Differencing the air-part [θ] between levels would compare different places: at 1000 hPa, 50°N in January the air is mostly the warm oceans', at 925 hPa it includes the cold continents, and N² came out −0.57, an unstable layer that isn't there. The column mean gives 1.54 (1.72 with the extrapolations). Without the extrapolated profiles the low-level air is more stable in July at 925 hPa, 60–75°S (1.6–2.9 → 2.1–4.2) and at 1000 hPa, 45–60°N (1.8 → 2.5–2.7), where the air at 1000 hPa is mostly over the oceans.

Reference: Boer (1982, MWR) for pressure levels below the surface.

Term
Budget closure here has not yet been formally validated against published atlases. Spatial patterns and sign conventions are canonical and reliable for pedagogy; for quantitative research use, cross-check magnitudes against a trusted reference before citing a number.

Meridional transport across each latitude circle, northward positive, in PW (10¹⁵ W).

Total (TOA) — absorbed solar minus OLR, integrated from the South Pole, with the global-mean imbalance removed so the curve closes at both poles:

T(φ) = ∫∫ (R_TOA − ⟨R_TOA⟩) dA   over the cap south of φ,   R_TOA = ASR − OLR

Atmosphere — ERA5's vertical integral of the total-energy flux ∫(c_pT + Φ + L_v q + k)·v dp/g, computed every hour on 137 model levels and integrated around the latitude circle. Being a monthly mean of hourly fluxes, it includes the transient eddies. Ocean = total − atmosphere, the residual method of Trenberth & Caron (2001).

Mass correction (Trenberth 1997). Reanalysis column mass fluxes don't satisfy the mass budget, and every 10⁹ kg s⁻¹ of spurious net mass flux across a latitude carries ≈0.3 PW at the column-mean energy (≈3.3×10⁵ J kg⁻¹). A vertically uniform (barotropic) wind is added so that the net mass flux equals what the budget allows — the water-vapour flux plus the climatological dry-mass tendency — and it carries the mean energy of the latitude circle's cross-section. The caption reports the size of the correction.

Decomposition of the atmospheric transport — the same [·] / * split as the H and Q budgets, here vertically integrated over masked, mass-weighted columns:

MMC        ∮∫ [v][s] dp/g        mean meridional circulation
stationary ∮∫ [v*s*] dp/g        zonal departures of the monthly mean
transient  = atmosphere − MMC − stationary      (s = c_pT + gz + L_v q)

MMC and stationary eddies come from the monthly-mean v, T, z, q on 12 pressure levels (levels below the surface excluded); MMC carries the same mass correction. "Transient" is a residual: sub-monthly eddies, plus the year-to-year covariance of the monthly means and the difference between 12 pressure levels and ERA5's 137 model levels — trust it where it is large (the storm tracks), not its small tropical wiggles. Seasonal and annual means average the monthly decompositions, so the seasonal migration of the Hadley cells stays in the MMC.

Moisture — the same with q in place of s, shown as latent energy L_v·F (1 PW ≙ 4.0×10⁸ kg s⁻¹ of water vapour ≈ 0.4 Sv). "E − P implied" integrates the surface evaporation minus precipitation (ERA5 short-range forecasts, E = −slhf / L_v) minus the column-water tendency, with its global-mean imbalance removed; its gap to the analysed vapour flux is the reanalysis moisture-budget residual.

Expected annual means (Trenberth & Caron 2001): atmosphere ≈5.0 PW at 43°N and similar near 40°S; the total peaks near 35° (≈5.8 PW in the NH), where the atmosphere carries 78 % (NH) and 92 % (SH) of it; the ocean dominates only between the equator and ≈17°N.

References: Trenberth (1997, J. Climate 10); Trenberth & Caron (2001, J. Climate 14); Trenberth & Stepaniak (2003, J. Climate 16); Peixoto & Oort (1992, Physics of Climate); Mayer & Haimberger (2012, J. Climate 25); Mayer, Mayer & Haimberger (2021, J. Climate 34) on ERA5 budgets.
